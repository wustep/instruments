# The stage (apps/web): Day Studio

Scope: the whole web app's look (stage, Orchestra, file player, sheet music, Measurements, Notes, 404).
Visitor mode: Experience (the instrument leads), with Operate controls round it and two Read pages.

Audience and job: someone sent a link, a musician, an audio person; untrained, minutes at a time, phone or
computer. One gesture to a sound; then the instrument seen whole and working.
Proof: the instrument itself, moving with the simulation. No claims.
Constraints: the product's rules in AGENTS.md; the engine, seating, addresses and behaviours untouched; no
theme picker; no new permanent copy on the stage.
Memorable moment: a note played in daylight: the object on a bright field, its string drawn in blue.
Unresolved: the link preview image still shows the old look; behaviour raised by the critique (undo, names on
touch, file errors) is the owner's call.

## Direction contract

THESIS: An instrument in a north-lit white studio at midday, seen whole. Day has no glows, so state has two
forms only: ink is what is chosen, blue is what sounds. Refuses the category's dark "pro audio" panel and the
light SaaS dashboard alike: no cards, no rails, no borders round regions.

OWN-WORLD: One cool plaster white for wall, floor and page (#eceff1), paper white for what is laid on it,
blue-black ink (#1c222a), one ultramarine (#1d48d1) kept for sound. Ysabeau Office, a Garamond-boned sans:
light and large for the readout, medium and small for labels; a mono only for code and equations. Objects cast
soft shadows down and to the right; nothing glows. Controls are pale, round, drawn: discs, pills, hairlines.

STORY: The visitor sees an object in good light, touches a key, and sees exactly what moved, marked in blue
and named in the readout. They change instrument, turn it, seat an orchestra, press play.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The instrument centred on the pale field with its shadow, the largest thing by far. Above it,
one row: sixteen instruments by family parted by space, the Orchestra as a mode at its end, the view and the
pages at the right. Under that row, on the wall with no panel, the readout at the left in light 40 px type,
the spectrum and the scope in ink. Top middle of the stage, the player: a small pale pill whose play button is
one solid ink disc (the primary action besides the keys). Under the stage, a pale fallboard (pedals, octave,
named knobs) and the keys.

FORM: a daylight studio (white cyc, north window, skylight); seed: none (pinned) — "A bright, minimal
daylight studio. High-key, pale ground, soft north light, the feeling of a quiet photography studio or a
modern practice room at midday."; "replace the dark-walled product studio with a bright cyc / daylight
environment: a large soft skylight or north-window key, gentle warm-white fill, almost no dramatic rim, soft
shadows, higher exposure."; "Pale surfaces, a small number of weights, one precise accent." Signature
interaction: a key pressed draws its string, its key and its name in the one blue. Motion grammar: 120–200 ms
ease-out on state, the instrument's own motion untouched, none under reduced motion. Build path: code-led (no
image generation in this session).

ADAPTATIONS (on purpose, recorded after the finish review): the vector style is a drawing on drafting film,
so it has a third face (Hershey Simplex capitals, a plotter's letters), a hairline frame round its sheet, and
three coloured leads (green, red, magenta, with a deep graphite) that tie a knob to the part of the drawing it
moves and to its handle there. The leads are a key the incumbent's function depends on, not an accent: they
appear nowhere outside the vector style. In 2D the grand's case is as wide as the keyboard under it (each
string stands over its key), so on a phone the board fills the stage and the plaster shows only at the bar;
the room is a patch of light under the instrument, not a drawn wall.
